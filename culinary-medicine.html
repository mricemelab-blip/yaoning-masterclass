About the Textbook

Culinary Medicine — University of Arizona, 2025

Culinary Medicine is a pioneering evidence-based textbook published by the University of Arizona. It integrates nutritional science with the culinary arts, providing healthcare professionals and wellness practitioners with a comprehensive knowledge system spanning from social dietary behaviors to clinical nutritional interventions.

The book comprises 12 chapters organized into four sections — from social determinants and meal planning, kitchen science and food safety, food psychology, macro- and micronutrients, dietary supplements, bioactive compounds, popular diet patterns, culinary demonstration design, food systems and community nutrition, to culturally responsive approaches. Appendices include clinical case studies and disease prevention recipes.

    Overview of the book's purpose, scope, and structure. Defines culinary medicine as a subdiscipline integrating nutrition evidence and culinary techniques. Describes the intended audiences and how to use the textbook.

Section I — Preparing, Selecting & Enjoying Nutritious Food
  • 01
    Understanding Social Influences, Planning Meals, and Shopping Smarter External
    Constance Bell; Stavroula N. Antonopoulos; Milad Hasankhani; Aimee Novak
    Explores how social determinants of health (SDOH) shape food access. Meal planning within limited resources, strategies for purchasing nutrient-dense foods, and smart shopping on a budget.
  • 02
    In the Kitchen — Food Storage and Preparation; Preventing Food Waste External
    Aimee Novak; Stavroula N. Antonopoulos; Milad Hasankhani
    General food safety principles, effective storage strategies, and preparation techniques — including whole grains, pulses, proteins, and nutrient-dense fats — to maximize health benefits and minimize waste.
  • 03
    Enjoying Food at Home and Beyond — Our Relationship with Food External
    Ashley Munro; Katelyn Barker; Hope Wilson; Jennifer Parlin; Constance Bell
    The psychology of eating, emotional eating, mindful eating practices, and building a healthy relationship with food while honoring its social and cultural significance. Emphasizes flexibility over rigid dietary rules.
Section II — Food as Medicine
  • 04
    Macronutrients External
    Katelyn Barker
    Biochemical functions and energy metabolism of carbohydrates, proteins, lipids, and water. Daily requirements, food sources, and applications in culinary medicine practice.
  • 05
    Micronutrients External
    Mary Marian; Bahar Bakhshi
    Functions, deficiency signs, food sources, and biomarkers of vitamins and minerals. How cooking and food processing affect micronutrient bioavailability.
  • 06
    Dietary Supplements External
    Mary Marian
    Safety and efficacy evaluation of dietary supplements. FDA regulatory framework, risks of misleading advertising, and interactions with prescription medications.
  • 07
    Bioactive Compounds External
    Amy A. Drescher
    Polyphenols, terpenes, organosulfur compounds, probiotics, omega-3 fatty acids, and isoflavones — their metabolic effects and health promotion mechanisms. Evidence-based examination of "superfoods" and bioavailability considerations.
  • 08
    Popular Diets and Health External
    Farshad Fani Marvasti
    Critical analysis of fad diets versus evidence-based eating patterns. Intervention evidence for Mediterranean diet, DASH diet, and other dietary approaches in chronic disease prevention.

    Anatomy of a Food Demonstration External
    Jennifer Parlin; Stavroula N. Antonopoulos; Aimee Novak; Constance E. Bell
    Design and execution of culinary demonstrations. Food safety protocols, audience analysis, cultural sensitivity, and effective nutrition communication across diverse community settings.
